Johns Hopkins University · AI Business Strategy

Prison Management System Modernization

A capstone strategy report for Johns Hopkins' AI Business Strategy certificate program: recovering a stalled $350M, five-year AI-enabled system.

Overview

What it is

The final project for the 10-week AI Business Strategy certificate program at Johns Hopkins' Whiting School of Engineering (completed March 2026). The assignment was a case scenario: a $350 million, five-year initiative to consolidate seven legacy systems into a single AI-enabled platform for inmate management, security compliance, and staffing, that had fallen behind schedule, over budget by $20M+, and losing client trust.

I wrote a full assessment and recovery strategy: diagnosing why the AI components were failing, proposing a 12-month recovery plan, and designing a five-year operations and recompete strategy to rebuild client confidence.

My approach

Diagnose, recover, sustain, measure

Diagnose

Root-cause analysis

Traced the failures across five structural dimensions — data governance, system architecture, AI operations, resourcing, and client governance — rather than treating them as isolated technical bugs.

Recover

Phased 12-month plan

Stabilize (0–3 months), optimize (3–9 months), then operational readiness (9–12 months), sequenced so each phase de-risks the next.

Sustain

Five-year O&M strategy

A recompete strategy built on transparent performance dashboards, quarterly client reviews, and an ongoing AI governance framework.

Measure

Defined KPIs

Concrete targets — prisoner-update latency under 5 minutes, recommender accuracy above 85%, security detection above 95%, staffing accuracy above 80%.

Note

What this is, and isn't

This was a graded capstone analyzing a program case scenario, not a live client engagement. I'm presenting it because it's the clearest evidence of how I approach a strategy problem: diagnose the real cause, sequence a realistic recovery, and attach numbers to every claim.
